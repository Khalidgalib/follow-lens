FollowLens — Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 2026

The short version: FollowLens does not collect, store, transmit, or share any personal data, from anyone, ever. The app has no internet access at all — it is physically incapable of sending your information anywhere, because that permission was never requested.

What FollowLens does

FollowLens is an offline utility that reads an Instagram data export you download yourself from Instagram's own "Download your information" tool, and shows you which accounts you follow that don't follow you back. That's the entire function of the app.

What data FollowLens accesses

When you choose to import a file, folder, or .zip export, FollowLens reads the follower and following lists inside it — usernames and, where available, timestamps — entirely on your device. FollowLens never asks for your Instagram username or password, and never logs into Instagram or any other service on your behalf.

Where your data goes

Nowhere but your own device. FollowLens:

  • Has no INTERNET permission on Android — this isn't a policy promise, it's a technical fact you can verify by checking the app's permissions.
  • Stores everything you import (snapshots, your whitelist, and accounts you've marked "handled") in a local SQLite database on your device only.
  • Sends nothing to the developer, to Instagram, to advertisers, or to any third party.
  • Contains no analytics SDKs, no crash reporters, no ad networks, and no trackers of any kind.

Deleting your data

Open FollowLens → Settings → "Clear all data" at any time to permanently delete every snapshot, whitelist entry, and handled marking from your device. Uninstalling the app has the same effect.

Accounts

FollowLens has no account system, no sign-up, and no login of any kind — there is nothing to register for and nothing tied to your identity.

Children's privacy

FollowLens does not knowingly collect data from anyone, including children, because it does not collect data from anyone, full stop.

Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes, the "Last updated" date above will change with it. Since the app collects nothing today, any future change would need to be a deliberate, disclosed addition of a new feature — not a quiet policy shift.
